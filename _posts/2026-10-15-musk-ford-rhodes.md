>I am not fully convinced by Slobodian and Tarnoff's concept of Muskism, particularly as a sequel to Fordism. If what they mean by Muskism is simply an analysis of Musk's ideology, that is fair enough as a descriptive exercise. But they appear to be attempting to equate Muskism with Fordism as a comparable historical rupture in productive organization, which is simply not credible.
>
>Fordism was a genuinely novel intervention in the organization of production, a systematic refinement of mechanized mass production into a replicable model that could be deployed throughout the industrial world to consistently generate productivity growth. The closest known attempt to move beyond that model was the Toyota Production System, which was itself not a complete break from Fordist principles but rather a significant internal refinement of them. Musk has not attempted to break with Fordism in any meaningful sense. He is more or less attempting to restore and maintain it, deploying economies of scale, vertical integration, and wage arbitrage rather than any new principle of productive organization.
>
>This is the fundamental flaw in Slobodian and Tarnoff's conception of Muskism as a rupture with Fordism: Musk has no new ideas about how to organize production. They appear to be confusing the novelty of his products with novelty in the organization of the productive process itself, which are categorically different things, and the conflation produces a misleading analytical framework.
